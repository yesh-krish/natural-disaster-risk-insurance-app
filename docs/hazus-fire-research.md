# Hazus and fire intensity: research assessment

Research checked November 3, 2026. **Recommendation: retain the existing wildfire sources; do not adopt Hazus as a wildfire-intensity provider.** No documented standalone wildfire-intensity model or suitable kW/m dataset was identified in current FEMA materials. Hazus offers a legacy **fire-following-earthquake (FFE)** methodology and useful exposure/consequence concepts, which answer different questions.

This assessment was documentation-only research; no Hazus analysis was run. Effort estimates below are engineering judgments. After this research, the user authorized [implementation of historical-weather potential surface-fire intensity](potential-fire-intensity.md). That follow-up uses Rothermel/Byram methods and public weather/fuel inputs, not Hazus, and does not estimate actual wildfire occurrence.

## Supported Hazus scope and the earthquake-fire distinction

FEMA's [program factsheet][F1] documents **earthquake, flood, hurricane, and tsunami** methods. This program scope must be separated from release availability. FEMA announced **Hazus 7.2 on June 4, 2026**, adding earthquake ShakeMap scenarios in ArcGIS Pro; 6.1 is unsupported following ArcGIS Desktop retirement. [FEMA announcement][F2]

The current [Map Service Center capability table][F3] lists flood analysis with supplied depth grids, hurricane analysis, and earthquake ShakeMap scenarios; tsunami is not yet available in 7.2. It lists annualized/probabilistic analysis only for flood. Hazus requires licensed ArcGIS Pro 3.4–3.6; its download uses reCAPTCHA. Hazus software itself is free. **FFE execution in 7.2 was not verified:** the legacy manual is evidence for the method, not proof of feature parity in the current release. See access limitations below.

The [6.1 earthquake user guidance, §§7.3.3 and 8.2.10, Table 8-8][F4] documents FFE ignitions, burned area, exposed people/inventory, and fire-water demand. A shaking event can create many urban ignitions while damaging water supply and limiting response. Inputs include wind, stations/trucks, and response assumptions. This is relevant to an **earthquake-induced urban conflagration** study. Wildfire instead requires vegetation fuels, moisture, terrain, weather, and ignition/spread context; urban and vegetation fires can interact, but the FFE method is not a validated substitution for wildland fire behavior.

For legacy FFE, exposed population/value are estimated from burned-area fraction times tract population/building value. **Exposed dollars are not automatically repair loss, and burned-area fraction is not burn severity or fire intensity.** [F4]

## Equations that are useful, with explicit quantity boundaries

**Fireline intensity:** `I = H × w × R`. With heat yield `H` in kJ/kg, fuel consumed in the flaming front `w` in kg/m², and perpendicular spread `R` in m/s, `I` is **kW/m**. If `R` is m/min, divide the product by 60. Total available fuel load is not automatically flaming consumption. This is the Byram relationship discussed in [Andrews et al. (2011)][R2]; it supplies a definition and calculation when inputs are known, not occurrence probability. Do not invert a surface-fire flame-length correlation for all fire types or transform a mean flame length into mean intensity.

**Surface spread:** Rothermel's relationship can be written `R = I_R ξ (1 + φ_w + φ_s) / (ρ_b ε Q_ig)`. In consistent SI units: reaction intensity `I_R` is kW/m²; propagating-flux ratio `ξ`, wind/slope factors `φ_w, φ_s`, and effective heating number `ε` are dimensionless; bulk fuel density `ρ_b` is kg/m³; ignition heat `Q_ig` is kJ/kg; `R` is m/s. Original implementations use other unit conventions. **Reaction intensity in kW/m² is distinct from Byram intensity in kW/m and from heat flux received by a structure.** [Rothermel (1972), Eq. 9][R1]

**FFE ignition, for comparison only:** Scawthorn's 2009 primary study gives `r = 0.581895 a² − 0.029444 a`, with PGA `a` in g and `r` in ignitions per million ft² of building floor area. Multiplying by floor area expressed in millions of ft² gives an expected count; the study describes uncertainty and Poisson sampling. Its reported fit is weak (`R² = 0.084`), and the polynomial becomes negative at low positive PGA. It is not an unrestricted operational formula or a confirmed 7.2 implementation. It estimates earthquake-conditioned ignitions, never wildfire intensity. [P1] (Eq. 3)

**Frequency:** compatible BP and conditional class probabilities can produce the app's research exceedance estimates; they cannot be combined with the unrelated TxWRAP mean to manufacture an annual intensity distribution. Annual kW/m exceedance requires compatible event/intensity distributions and occurrence weights. Likewise, converting FRP MW to kW/m would require justified radiant fraction, contributing front length, geometry and timing; unit conversion alone cannot do it. [FSim methodology][R3], [FRP experiments][R4]

## One practical next step

Target a **United States annual fireline-intensity exceedance curve**, with a small data extract used first to verify access and calculation. The completed [FSim follow-up](fsim-fireline-exceedance.md) identifies RDS-2025-0006 as the strongest event-data lead: its metadata advertises per-fire flame length and arrival time, while inspection of the actual public archive finds aggregate probability rasters and summaries, without native intensity events. Obtain a producer extract with native kW/m, event/year/pyrome identity and annual weights; verify it before implementation. The 2025 release is CONUS-only, so Alaska/Hawaii require compatible additional coverage. Perimeters or mean intensity alone cannot supply the curve.

The desired quantity is `P(maximum site fireline intensity during a year ≥ i)`, for thresholds `i` in kW/m. With complete equally weighted synthetic years, estimate it by the fraction of simulated years with at least one site exceedance, retaining non-burning years in the denominator and counting a year only once. Other event weighting requires its documented occurrence model. A conditional mean and annual burn probability cannot recover this distribution.

The [USFS FSim project description][U4] explicitly targets annual burning probabilities and intensity distributions and describes synthetic weather years and event sets. This establishes a methodological lead, not availability of all needed fields. Public [Wildfire Risk to Communities downloads][U5] offer annual BP, conditional mean flame length, and conditional 4-/8-ft exceedance layers. Those can improve flame-length analysis but do not independently fulfill a non-flame-length intensity objective. TxWRAP remains useful scenario context; accuracy still requires validation against appropriate observations.

## Professor-ready findings and open questions

**Finding:** Hazus is principally useful here for exposure/consequence methods and a separate earthquake-fire research question. Its documented legacy FFE outputs do not meet the app's wildfire-intensity requirement. The existing TxWRAP layer supplies modeled kW/m but does not satisfy the clarified goal of annual intensity exceedance. FSim distribution/event data are the stronger next research lead, with access and sufficient output detail still unverified. Rothermel/Byram methods support scenario analysis; FRP and burn severity remain independently named quantities.

**Open questions:** (1) Is legacy FFE executable in 7.2, and which assumptions changed? (2) What are TxWRAP's effective model resolution, vintage, update policy and local validation evidence? (3) Do the app's FSim conditional probabilities support an annual-maximum interpretation where repeat burns occur? (4) Can matched observed intensity or defensible event catalogs support validation and annual kW/m exceedance? These gaps do not justify adopting Hazus for wildfire intensity.

**Evidence limits:** direct FEMA/MSC requests sometimes returned 403 or timed out. Official indexed manuals and the accessible FEMA June 2026 bulletin were consulted; current MSC capability/access details were readable through a search-accessible cached rendering of the official page, not a downloaded 7.2 package. Therefore tsunami/current-platform statements carry that access limitation, and FFE feature parity remains unconfirmed. Some indexed PDFs exposed incomplete body text. No software was installed, credentials requested, or external contacts messaged. Existing October 29 validation artifacts were read as historical software checks, not fresh scientific validation.
